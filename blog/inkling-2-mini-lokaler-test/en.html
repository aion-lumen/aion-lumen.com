A new player in town: Inkling 2 Mini vs. Qwen and GLM.

For anyone building and running local AI, something happened last week that got buried in the usual storm of AI and world news: with Inkling 2 Small and the quickly following Mini release (26B-A5B), Mira Murati and her team gifted the community a genuinely capable model (open weights, Apache 2.0) in a space lately dominated almost entirely by Chinese releases.

Afschin Mirhamed · Mac Studio (96 GB)

I tested Inkling 2 Mini locally against Qwen3.8 27B and GLM-4.7 Flash in my internal Folio-Bench. Two concrete everyday tasks were evaluated: triaging emails (action required vs. archive) and answering questions accurately from document packets.

1 · Output room turns Inkling's result around

Under my existing 220-token cap, Inkling looked weak because its internal thinking phase consumed the output budget and truncated nearly a third of its answers. At 1,024 tokens with everything else unchanged, Inkling moved ahead of both Qwen and GLM on mail triage.

The two strongest models complement each other well: Inkling clearly led Qwen on older archive mails (78% vs. 53%), while Qwen matched the reference more often on recent mails (93% vs. 85%). Following a separate operational check, Inkling 2 Mini has replaced GLM since 22 November as the first check voice in my Workshop. Qwen stays primary.

2 · Even at 92%, a real citation alone is not enough

A single mistake by the strongest model delivered the most useful lesson: a valid source does not protect against a wrong conclusion. In synthetic tests, a missing payment receipt turned into an unpaid invoice, and an attendance confirmation turned into a missing certification. Inkling also once stretched a real number beyond its actual scope.

I therefore separate every AI response into three distinct checks:

  1. 01

    Verify the source

    Did the model actually see the cited document?

    Technically verifiable.
  2. 02

    Check the scope

    Does the source support this exact claim, including date and denominator?

    Requires content review.
  3. 03

    Keep status honest

    Is a claim proven, refuted, or still open?

    Actions follow their own approval rules.

Thank you, Thinking Machines

Having a genuinely strong, Apache-2.0 US model running right on my desk, keeping pace with Qwen and GLM and even moving ahead in this test with no API meter ticking: a year ago I would hardly have believed it.

A heartfelt thank you to Mira Murati and the entire Thinking Machines team for Inkling 2 Mini under Apache 2.0. Please keep them coming!

Methodology, sample and limits
  • Hardware & runtimes: Mac Studio (96 GB unified memory). Compared Qwen3.8 27B (MLX 4-bit in LM Studio) and Inkling 2 Mini 26B-A5B (NVFP4 in a local Ollama runtime), temperature 0.1, seed 17, 16,384 context tokens.
  • Mail test (60 cases): Curated selection of real mail cases (27 recent, 33 older): 20 prior action conflicts, 10 archiving variants, 10 domain variants, and 20 historical Qwen agreements. Not a representative random sample. One older case lacked a decisive attachment (accuracy denominator: 59; 32 older, 27 recent).
  • Reuse: Qwen (220 tokens) and Inkling (220 tokens) come unchanged from the first comparison run; only Inkling was re-run at 1,024 tokens. Qwen already delivered 60/60 complete answers at 220 tokens.
  • Source questions (24 cases): 24 questions across 18 source families on fixed source packets (no autonomous full-archive search), graded blind to model names. Invoice/receipt and attendance/certification examples come from separate synthetic test files.
  • Evaluation & privacy: All compared models ran locally. References and grading were prepared with Codex (not an independent human blind study). Only aggregate figures are published; personal mail content stays private.
  • Operational check: Before switching on 22 November 2026, a separate operational check verified formatting, phishing handling, abort/recovery, and backups. Local models remain fallible.
