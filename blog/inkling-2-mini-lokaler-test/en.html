Inkling 2 Mini
vs. Qwen and GLM.

In my recent local model tests, Chinese releases such as Qwen and GLM took centre stage. That is what makes Inkling 2 Mini (26B-A5B) from Thinking Machines so interesting: a compact, genuinely open Apache-2.0 US model that, in my view, hasn't received nearly enough credit yet.

Afschin Mirhamed

I tested it locally on my Mac Studio (96 GB) against Qwen3.8 27B and GLM-4.7 Flash for everyday mail triage in Folio, followed by a source-checking comparison with Qwen.

1 · Enough output room makes the difference

The setup: 60 curated real mail cases (59 with an unambiguous action reference). The compared models ran locally; only aggregate results are published. References and evaluation were prepared with Codex.

At first, Inkling looked unexpectedly weak. Under my existing 220-token output cap, Qwen completed 60 of 60 responses cleanly (42/59 matching actions). Inkling truncated 19 times mid-response—only 41/60 complete and 37/59 matching—because its internal thinking phase shares the output budget.

Re-running Inkling on the same cases at 1,024 tokens, with its other settings unchanged, changed the picture:

  • Complete responses: from 41/60 to 60/60
  • Matching actions: from 37/59 to 48/59—ahead of Qwen (42/59) and a repaired GLM-4.7-Flash candidate (41/59)

The split turned out to be complementary: Inkling clearly outperformed Qwen on older archive mails, while Qwen matched the reference more often on recent cases. Following a separate operational check, Inkling 2 Mini has replaced GLM as the first check voice since 22 November in my Workshop. Qwen remains my primary model.

2 · Where even strong local models still need brakes

A second comparison across 24 source-grounded questions delivered an equally important lesson: a valid source does not guarantee a valid conclusion.

I had already seen this pattern in synthetic document tests—a missing payment receipt turned into an unpaid invoice, or an attendance confirmation turned into a missing certification. In the new source questions too, Inkling cited genuine evidence yet once extended a claim beyond its actual scope.

In daily use, I therefore keep three checks distinct:

  1. 01

    Verify the source

    Did the model actually receive the cited source?

    Largely checkable mechanically.
  2. 02

    Check the scope

    Does the source support this exact claim, including date and denominator?

    Requires content review.
  3. 03

    Keep status honest

    Is a claim proven, refuted, or still open?

    Source checks support decisions; concrete actions follow their own approval rules.

A heartfelt thank you

Seeing a capable, Apache-2.0 US open-weights model run right on my desk—holding its own in these tests against Qwen and GLM with no paid model API meter ticking—still feels remarkable.

A heartfelt thank you to Mira Murati and the entire Thinking Machines team for releasing Inkling 2 Mini under Apache 2.0. More of this, please!

  • Hardware & setup: Mac Studio (96 GB unified memory). Compared Qwen3.8 27B (MLX 4-bit in LM Studio) and Inkling 2 Mini 26B-A5B (NVFP4 in a local Ollama runtime), temperature 0.1, seed 17, 16,384 context tokens. GLM-4.7 Flash used MLX 6-bit weights with a repaired short-answer template and neutral repetition penalty. Runtime and quantisation differ between models; this is a comparison of local configurations.
  • Mail sample (60 cases): Curated real mail cases (27 recent, 33 older): 20 prior action-conflict cases, 10 archiving variants, 10 domain variants, and 20 historical Qwen agreement cases. Not a representative random inbox sample. One older case lacked a decisive attachment (action denominator: 59; 32 older, 27 recent).
  • Reuse & subsets: Qwen · 220 and Inkling · 220 come unchanged from the prior run; only Inkling was re-run at 1,024 tokens. Breakdown at 1,024 tokens: older cases Inkling 25/32 vs. Qwen 17/32; recent cases Qwen 25/27 vs. Inkling 23/27. All 19 truncations at 220 tokens remain counted as technical failures.
  • Source questions (24 cases): 24 questions across 18 source families using fixed prepared packets (not autonomous full-archive search). Blind grading without visible model names: Inkling 22/24 directly usable vs. Qwen 16/24 (specific to this test, not a general ranking). Invoice/receipt and attendance/certification examples come from separate synthetic test files.
  • Evaluation & privacy: Compared models ran locally; references and grading were prepared with Codex (not an independent human blind study). Only aggregate figures are published; personal mail content and raw data stay private.
  • Operational limits: Inkling misclassified a phishing-like message in the comparison above. Before switching on 22 November 2026, the integration was adjusted on known cases and a separate operational check covered formatting, phishing handling, abort/recovery, and backups. That later operational check is not an independent replication of the 48/59 comparison. Local models remain fallible; source checks support decisions while concrete actions follow their own approval rules.
